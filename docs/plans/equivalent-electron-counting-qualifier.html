§1 — A count that is not a count of atoms

The Data Dictionary stores gas-injection inventories and rates in equivalent electrons: each injected atom or molecule contributes as many units as it carries electrons. The published catalog names these quantities as plain counts and rates. accumulated_argon_count is defined as 18 units per argon atom, lithium_source_rate_due_to_gas_injection as 3 per atom, accumulated_ammonia_count as 10 per molecule. A reader of the name expects atoms and is wrong by the atomic number.

A name-against-definition reading of the 1,977 names published in catalog release candidate v0.4.0rc8 found 64 such names. They are listed, with the phrase each definition uses, as the findings carrying the equivalent-electron-count family in /home/ITER/mcintos/Code/imas-codex/docs/evidence/fragments/sn-definitions-by-construction/name-definition-seat-catalog.json (imas-codex 4da5d1034). The lead ruled on 2026-11-13 that the definitions stay, because they describe the stored data, and the names are regenerated to state the convention.

The grammar cannot spell that today. On 2026-11-13 imas-codex's signed rename refused accumulated_argon_equivalent_electron_count: the spelling matches no physical base. The lead authorised a new token and a grammar release the same day. Of the 64 names, 42 are counts (…_count, …_prefill_count) and 22 are rates (…_source_rate), so one word must serve both.

§2 — One word for the equivalent-electron convention

The grammar gains the vocabulary a name needs to say that its count or rate is expressed in equivalent electrons. It is one addition that composes with the existing count and source-rate bases and with the species, accumulation, prefill and process tokens those names already use. It is not a new base per quantity. The lead ruled the spelling on 2026-11-13: the word reads equivalent_electron and is the word next to the base, after any other word that forms the kind of count, so the argon inventory is accumulated_argon_equivalent_electron_count, a prefill inventory is neon_prefill_equivalent_electron_count, and the lithium rate is lithium_equivalent_electron_source_rate_due_to_gas_injection. The repository already records this convention under the spelling electron_equivalent, as a transformation its tests expect to be absent; that record is retired in the same change, so one convention has one spelling and no test asserts the feature does not exist. The change adds one public vocabulary word and no refusal: no word of this kind is restricted to particular bases today, and this one is not made the exception.

The word is a unit convention, which none of the grammar's present presentation groups for such words names, so the group it is filed under is stated in the change and in the landing record.

Done when:

  • each of these six names parses, renders back to the same string, and reports the equivalent-electron convention as a distinct part of its parse rather than as part of a species or a base: accumulated_argon_equivalent_electron_count, accumulated_ammonia_equivalent_electron_count, neon_prefill_equivalent_electron_count, accumulated_oxygen_prefill_equivalent_electron_count_due_to_gas_injection, lithium_equivalent_electron_source_rate_due_to_gas_injection, total_equivalent_electron_source_rate_at_wall_due_to_gas_injection;
  • the six plain names they replace still parse as they do at the base revision, and no name in the grammar's own round-trip corpus changes its parse;
  • a test fails when the word is removed from the vocabulary, and the spelling with the two words in the other order, neon_equivalent_electron_prefill_count, is not what the grammar renders for that parse;
  • no test in the suite expects the convention to be absent, and electron_equivalent is not accepted as a second spelling of it;
  • the word's definition (one unit per electron carried by the injected atom or molecule) is held once, where the grammar holds the definitions of its conventions, and the word is registered at every site the grammar's own invariant tests require of a word of its kind, with those tests passing;
  • the grammar test suite passes on a compute partition with no failure added against the base revision, in a committed log ending with its exit status.

Once §2 lands, a release candidate is cut from main with the release CLI, which the coordinator runs from the main checkout after CI is green on upstream; the CLI refuses a detached worktree. The lead authorised the release and the push of main to upstream on 2026-11-13. imas-codex then bumps both pins in its pyproject.toml and syncs its graph grammar snapshot under sn-definitions-by-construction.

Done when: a tag above v0.11.1 carrying §2 is pushed to upstream, its publish workflow is green, and PyPI lists that version; the six names of §2 parse under the published package installed from PyPI, not from the checkout.
